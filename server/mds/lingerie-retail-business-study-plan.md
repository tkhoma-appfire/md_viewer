## How to Use This Plan

**Goal:** Build both *business competence* and *lingerie-specific expertise* before you invest heavily in inventory, lease, or marketing.

**Recommended approach:**

- Study **business fundamentals first** (months 1–2)
- Add **lingerie-specific knowledge** in parallel (fit, sizing, product categories)
- Layer on **retail operations and e-commerce** (months 2–4)
- Finish with **marketing, finance, and scaling** (months 4–6)

**Time commitment:** 8–12 hours/week is realistic for meaningful progress alongside a day job.

|-------|-------|----------|---------|

## What to Study (By Topic)

### 1. Market Research & Positioning

- Lingerie market segments: everyday basics, fashion lingerie, bridal, maternity, post-surgery, plus-size, sustainable
- Competitor analysis (local boutiques + online brands in your region)
- Customer personas: age, price sensitivity, fit pain points, shopping preferences (online vs in-store)
- Niche selection: *who* you serve matters more than *what* you sell at the start

**Study outputs:** One-page positioning statement, 3 competitor profiles, ideal customer avatar

---

### 2. Lingerie Product Knowledge (Critical for Retail)

Unlike general fashion, lingerie retail lives or dies on **fit expertise**.

| Category | What to learn |
|----------|---------------|
| **Bras** | Band/cup sizing, sister sizes, wire vs wireless, balconette, plunge, full-cup, sports bras |
| **Panties** | Cuts (brief, bikini, thong, high-waist), fabrics, size ranges |
| **Sleepwear & loungewear** | Seasonal buying, fabric care |
| **Shapewear** | Compression levels, sizing vs regular apparel |
| **Specialty** | Mastectomy, maternity/nursing, teen first bra, post-surgery |

**Key stat to understand:** Industry estimates suggest 70–80% of women wear the wrong bra size — expert fitting is a major differentiator.

---

### 3. Bra Fitting (Non-Negotiable for Physical Retail)

Study:

- Fit-by-sight vs tape measure methods
- Signs of poor fit (gapping, spillage, riding band, slipping straps)
- How to fit without having every size in stock
- Specialty fittings: maternity, post-surgery, teens
- Privacy, dignity, and inclusive fitting room practices

**Certification recommended** if you plan a physical boutique (see [Specialized Training](#specialized-training-beyond-udemy)).

---

### 4. Business Planning & Legal

- Business structures (sole proprietorship, LLC, etc. — varies by country)
- Business plan: executive summary, market analysis, financial projections
- Permits, sales tax/VAT, import duties (if sourcing abroad)
- Return/exchange policies for intimate apparel (hygiene regulations vary — study local law)
- Insurance: general liability, product liability, workers' comp

---

### 5. Sourcing & Inventory

- Wholesale vs private label vs consignment
- MOQs (minimum order quantities), lead times, seasonal calendars
- Fabric quality: lace, mesh, cotton, modal, elastane blends
- Quality control: stitching, hardware, wash durability
- Open-to-buy (OTB) budgeting — how much inventory to buy per season
- Dead stock management (lingerie has higher return rates than many categories)

**Fashion industry calendar:** Spring/Summer and Fall/Winter collections; learn buying seasons 6–9 months ahead.

---

### 6. Pricing & Finance

- Cost of goods sold (COGS), markup, margin
- Keystone markup (2x wholesale) as a starting benchmark — adjust for positioning
- Break-even analysis
- Cash flow (inventory ties up capital)
- Retail KPIs: sell-through rate, inventory turnover, average transaction value, conversion rate

---

### 7. Retail Operations (Physical Store)

- Location analysis: foot traffic, demographics, rent as % of revenue
- Store layout: fitting rooms (critical), privacy, lighting
- POS systems and inventory management
- Staff training: fit, upselling, customer service
- Visual merchandising for intimate apparel

---

### 8. E-Commerce

- Platform choice: Shopify (most common for fashion), WooCommerce, etc.
- Size guides and fit content (reduces returns)
- Photography: on-model vs flat lay; modesty and brand tone
- Shipping, packaging (discreet packaging is often expected)
- Return policy design (balance customer trust vs hygiene/cost)

---

### 9. Marketing & Brand

- Brand identity: name, tone, visual language (sensual vs comfort-focused vs inclusive)
- Content marketing: fit education builds trust
- Social media: Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest (visual platforms dominate)
- Influencer partnerships (authenticity matters in lingerie)
- Email marketing and loyalty programs
- Community building (fit workshops, bra fitting events)

---

### 10. Customer Experience

- Inclusive sizing and body-positive messaging
- Discreet, judgment-free fitting experiences
- Loyalty and repeat purchase drivers (bras have replacement cycles)
- Handling returns and exchanges with empathy and clear policy

## Books to Read

Organized by priority. Start with **Tier 1**, then branch based on your model (boutique vs online-first).

### Tier 1 — Start Here

| Book | Author | Why read it |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **How to Be a Lingerie Designer** (also valuable for retailers) | Van Jonsson | Lingerie-specific roadmap: manufacturing, sizing, grading, pricing, marketing, finances. Closest thing to a lingerie startup bible. |
| **The Fashion Entrepreneur** | Keanan Duffty | Legal structure, business plan, supply chain, scaling. Interviews with industry leaders (Tommy Hilfiger, etc.). |
| **Profit First** | Mike Michalowicz | Cash flow management — essential when inventory eats capital. |

### Tier 2 — Fashion Business Depth

| Book | Author | Why read it |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Fashion Entrepreneurship: Principles and Practice** | Caro Tan & Islam | Comprehensive textbook: Lean Startup, sustainable sourcing, e-commerce, legal/financial considerations. |
| **Selling Fashion Collections** | Jessel Brizan | Buyer meetings, line sheets, lookbooks, wholesale — critical if you plan to sell to other retailers later. |
| **The Lean Startup** | Eric Ries | MVP thinking: test niche and assortment before scaling inventory. |

### Tier 3 — Retail Operations & Marketing

| Book | Author | Why read it |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Retail Business Kit for Dummies** | Rick Segel | Practical retail operations, staffing, customer service. |
| **Buyology** | Martin Lindstrom | Consumer psychology — useful for merchandising and marketing. |
| **Building a StoryBrand** | Donald Miller | Clarify your brand message (comfort, confidence, inclusivity, luxury). |
| **This Is Marketing** | Seth Godin | Niche marketing and trust-building for sensitive product categories. |

### Tier 4 — E-Commerce & Digital

| Book | Author | Why read it |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **E-commerce Evolved** | Tanner Larsson | Store optimization, conversion, traffic. |
| **Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook** | Gary Vaynerchuk | Social media content strategy. |
| **Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion** | Robert Cialdini | Ethical persuasion for sales and marketing. |

### Tier 5 — Optional Deep Dives

| Book | Author | Why read it |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **The End of Fashion** | Teri Agins | Industry context and how fashion retail has changed. |
| **Deluxe: How Luxury Lost Its Luster** | Dana Thomas | If targeting premium/luxury lingerie positioning. |
| **Women from the Ankle Down** | Rachelle Bergstein | Cultural history of intimate apparel — useful for brand storytelling. |

## Suggested 6-Month Study Schedule

### Month 1 — Foundation

- [ ] Read: *The Fashion Entrepreneur* (first half)
- [ ] Udemy: **Online Fashion Startup Case Studies**
- [ ] Complete 10+ competitor shop visits (online and/or in person)
- [ ] Define niche, target customer, and price positioning
- [ ] Draft one-page business concept

### Month 2 — Product & Fit

- [ ] Read: *How to Be a Lingerie Designer* (focus: sizing, grading, collection development chapters)
- [ ] Enroll in bra fitting course (Pudding, Esteem, or equivalent)
- [ ] Practice fit concepts on friends/family (with consent)
- [ ] Build size/fit FAQ for your future store

### Month 3 — Finance & Buying

- [ ] Udemy: **Retail Financial Planning**
- [ ] Read: *Profit First*
- [ ] Create Open-to-Buy spreadsheet for first season
- [ ] Contact 5–10 potential suppliers; request wholesale terms
- [ ] Udemy: **Boutique Basics** (if physical store)

### Month 4 — E-Commerce & Operations

- [ ] Udemy: **eCommerce Empire** or **Shopify Masterclass**
- [ ] Set up Shopify trial store (even as practice)
- [ ] Write return policy, privacy policy, size guide
- [ ] Read: *Building a StoryBrand*

### Month 5 — Marketing & Brand

- [ ] Read: *This Is Marketing*
- [ ] Create brand guide (colors, tone, photography direction)
- [ ] Udemy: Instagram/Facebook ads course
- [ ] Plan launch content (30 posts worth of ideas)
- [ ] Identify 10 micro-influencers in your niche

### Month 6 — Business Plan & Pre-Launch

- [ ] Complete full business plan with 12-month financial projections
- [ ] Finalize supplier orders (start small — test assortment)
- [ ] Soft launch: friends/family, pop-up, or limited online release
- [ ] Measure: conversion rate, average order value, return rate, fit-related feedback
- [ ] Read: *Fashion Entrepreneurship* (selected chapters as reference)

## Key Metrics to Track While Learning

Even before launch, practice thinking in retail metrics:

| Metric | What it means | Target (general guide) |
|--------|---------------|------------------------|
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue | 50–65% for lingerie retail |
| **Sell-through rate** | Units sold / Units received | 60–70%+ per season |
| **Inventory turnover** | COGS / Average inventory | Higher is better; lingerie often 2–4x/year |
| **Conversion rate** | Purchases / Store or site visits | 20–40% in-store with good fit service; 1–3% online |
| **Return rate** | Returns / Orders | Plan for 10–25% online (fit issues); lower in-store with fitting |
| **Average transaction value** | Total revenue / Number of transactions | Increase via bundling (bra + panty sets) |

- [ ] Can confidently fit a customer in a bra (certification recommended)

- [ ] Have a 12-month cash flow projection
- [ ] Know local regulations for intimate apparel returns/hygiene

### Business

- [ ] Business registered; tax and permits in place
- [ ] Business bank account
- [ ] Insurance quoted or purchased
- [ ] Wholesale accounts or inventory secured

### Customer-Facing

- [ ] Size guide published
- [ ] Return/exchange policy clear and legal
- [ ] Fitting room privacy standards set (physical store)
- [ ] Packaging plan (discreet shipping for online)

### Marketing

- [ ] Brand identity complete
- [ ] Website or store ready
- [ ] Launch campaign planned
- [ ] Email capture and welcome sequence

## Final Advice

1. **Start narrow.** One clear niche (e.g., "inclusive everyday bras D–G cup" or "luxury sleepwear") beats a generic lingerie shop.
2. **Fit is your moat.** In a world of online giants, expert fitting and trust win loyal customers.
3. **Inventory is risk.** Buy conservatively for season 1; let sell-through data guide season 2.
4. **Returns are normal.** Budget for them; reduce them with fit content and good sizing guidance.
5. **Learn from operators.** Follow boutique owners on Instagram/LinkedIn; attend local retail meetups.
